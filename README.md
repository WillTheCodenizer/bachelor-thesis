4.1 Fisher Matrix
The Fisher matrix allows one to easily manipulate parameter constraints over several
variables as it measures how sensitive the data is to the parameters. It is therefore
a method for determining the sensitivity of a particular measurement to a set of pa-
rameters and is extensively used in cosmological forecasting. Considering the fact
that conducting experiments often comes with a certain effort, forecasting can help to
optimise design decisions for a future experiment.

positive for cosmic shear and the cross correlation. However, all probes as well as the
cross correlation suggest that H0 and σ8 are negatively correlated.
When it comes to the FRBs, it is notable that the sensitivity for the measurement of H0
it greater than the sensitivity of cosmic shear. This is one the one hand due to the fact
that weak lensing measures angles, i.e. ratios of lengths. Since the Hubble constant itself
acts on length scales, the measurement of it via cosmic shear is less effective than via
e.g. DM clustering which is the basis for the FRBs. Furthermore the FRB correlation
depends on H0 via its weight function as given in equation 15. In contrast to that,
the FRB correlation is not as sensitive for the measurement of Ωm. While the cosmic
shear correlation is directly connected to the mass density parameter via its weight
function as given in equation 24, this is not the case for the FRBs. As both weight func-
tions are part of the cross correlation, it is therefore directly sensitive to both H0 and Ωm.
The cross correlation of FRBs with probes of the large-scale structure such as cosmic
shear as used in this thesis shows great potential for future surveys. The DM clustering
can compete with the more conventional probe of weak gravitational lensing, especially
when investigating the constraints on the Hubble constant or similar parameters. It is
noteworthy that for FRBs a relatively small sample of sources is already sufficient to
conduct a successful analysis compared to cosmic shear, for which millions of sources
are required.

5 Summary & Outlook
The purpose of this thesis was to study the power spectra of FRBs and cosmic shear as
well as the cross correlation of the two probes in order to investigate how well baryonic
feedback could be measured. The angular correlation functions were calculated in
astropy using the hmf module. The impact of shape noise was taken into account for
both FRBs and cosmic shear. Assuming Gaussian likelihoods, the parameter constraints
were obtained via the calculation of the corresponding Fisher matrices for a fiducial flat
ΛCDM cosmology.
The angular correlation functions were calculated based on the Limber approximation.
The plots follow the course of the nonlinear power spectrum. However, white noise
dominates the FRB correlation for multipoles ℓ≳ 100, the same is the case for cosmic
shear and the corresponding shape noise caused by the intrinsic ellipticities and finite
number of the galaxies. As expected, the order of magnitude of the cross correlation is
between those of the FRB and cosmic shear correlations.
It was found that the correlation function of FRBs was similarly effective in constraining
parameters as the cosmic shear correlation, especially when taking the Hubble constant
into account which cannot be effectively constrained by cosmic shear alone. A major
advantage was that for most parameter combinations there was a negative correlation in
the FRBs while the cosmic shear analysis brought forth a positive correlation, and vice
versa. Moreover, the constraints were considerably more limited by the cross correlation.
This can be inferred from the marginalised errors in Table 3 as well as the confidence
ellipses depicted in Figure 15.
The direct sensitivities of the correlation functions to certain parameter variations lead
to corresponding results for the confidence ellipses. However, there may also be indirect
effects. As mentioned in section 4, the analysis conducted in this thesis is far from final
and could be improved in precision.
• The electron bias was set to be = 1 for the sake of simplification even though the
true electron bias is presumably not constant.
• As the analysis presented here is based on the Fisher formalism, possible non-
Gaussian contributions to the covariance were not taken into account.
• A flat ΛCDM-model was adopted as a fiducial model. The variation of the fiducial
model will likely have an impact on the results obtained.
Including some of the points mentioned above and in section 4 could significantly
improve the constraints on the parameters. Nonetheless the results obtained in the
course of this thesis highlight the benefits of using cross correlations of different probes
of the large-scale structure. Given these results, there are promising prospects for future
investigations of baryonic feedback based on the cross correlation of FRBs with other
probes of the cosmic large-scale structure.28 5 Summary & Outlook
All in all, the cross correlation of FRBs with cosmic shear has great potential for
future investigations. The FRB correlation works for a relatively small sample of
sources. Furthermore, the FRB correlation serves as a complement to weak gravita-
tional lensing, especially in terms of its sensitivity to the measurement of the Hubble
constant. This makes the cross correlation of the two probes a powerful tool to investi-
gate constraints on cosmological parameters.
With regard to future investigations it would be interesting to explore the impact
of the choice of the fiducial model, to modify the electron bias in the modelling of
the dispersion measure or to use real FRB data to find constraints on cosmological
parameters and therefore on baryonic feedback.
