\chapter{Introduction} \label{ch:introduction}

Fast radio bursts (FRBs) are bright radio transients with millisecond durations that originate at
cosmological distances~\cite{Petroff_2019}. Since the first burst was found in archival pulsar
survey data~\cite{Lorimer_2007}, detections have grown rapidly, driven by the Canadian Hydrogen 
Intensity Mapping Experiment (CHIME)~\cite{Amiri2018}, whose second catalog contains over $3600$ 
unique sources~\cite{CHIMEFRB2026}.
Beyond the open questions regarding their nature, FRBs are emerging as a novel cosmological probe.
Their dispersion measure (DM) traces the diffuse ionized baryons, which are otherwise difficult to
observe~\cite{McQuinn_2014}, and has been used to measure the cosmic baryon
density~\cite{Macquart_2020} and the Hubble constant~\cite{Hagstotz_2022}. Larger samples 
could also constrain primordial non-Gaussianity~\cite{Reischke_2021} and baryonic feedback~\cite{Sharma_2025}.

The astrophysical origin of FRBs, however, remains unresolved. A burst from the Galactic magnetar
SGR 1935+2154 established a link to magnetars~\cite{CHIMEFRB_2020}, whereas FRBs in old stellar
environments point toward additional, delayed formation channels~\cite{Wang_2020, Petroff_2022}.
Identifying individual host galaxies requires arcsecond localizations, which exist for only a small
fraction of the population~\cite{RafieiRavandi_2020}. A statistical alternative exploits that FRBs
trace the large-scale structure~\cite{MasuiSigurdson_2015}, so that their angular clustering encodes
the bias of the host population and thereby the progenitor channel~\cite{Shirasaki_2017}. As FRB
samples are sparse, their auto-correlation is dominated by shot noise, which cross-correlation with
dense galaxy samples can mitigate. Such an FRB$\times$galaxy cross-correlation has already been detected in
the first CHIME/FRB catalog~\cite{RafieiRavandi_2021}. Photometric galaxy surveys like the Kilo-Degree
Survey (KiDS), whose final data release KiDS-Legacy provides calibrated redshift distributions in
six tomographic bins, are particularly well suited for this purpose~\cite{Wright_2025, Wright_2024}.

In this thesis, a forecasting pipeline is developed to assess how well the angular cross-correlation
between FRBs and KiDS-Legacy galaxies can constrain the clustering bias of the FRB host population.
Two progenitor scenarios are considered, namely young magnetars promptly following the cosmic star-formation history
and neutron stars formed through delayed channels, each with a redshift-dependent linear
bias. They are combined with a shallow, CHIME-like FRB survey as well as a deep FRB survey modeled on the
Square Kilometre Array (SKA)~\cite{Hashimoto_2020}.
Angular auto- and cross-power spectra are computed in the Limber approximation and propagated into a
Fisher forecast, which quantifies the gain of the joint analysis over the FRB auto-correlation alone
and the prospects of distinguishing the two progenitor scenarios.

The structure of this thesis is as follows: \autoref{ch:theoretical_background} reviews the
cosmological background, the properties and possible progenitors of FRBs, and the statistical
description of the large-scale structure. \autoref{ch:methodology} describes the modeling of both
tracer populations and the computation of the angular power spectra. The Fisher forecast is
presented and discussed in \autoref{ch:results}, while \autoref{ch:conclusion} summarizes the
findings and gives an outlook on future, wider galaxy surveys.
